# Three-point lighting

A scene with a single source looks flat. The classic arrangement fixes most of
it before you touch a single material.

## The three lights

The **key** is the main one, about 45 degrees off the object and a little above
the eye line. It decides where the light comes from and where shadows land.

The **fill** sits opposite, at half or a third of the key's strength. It is not
there to remove the shadow — it is there so the shadow stops being black.

The **rim** comes from behind, pointing at the camera. That is what separates
the object from the background and gives back the sense of volume.

## The common mistake

Turning the key up when the image looks dull. The problem is rarely a lack of
light; it is a lack of contrast between the lit and the shadowed areas.
